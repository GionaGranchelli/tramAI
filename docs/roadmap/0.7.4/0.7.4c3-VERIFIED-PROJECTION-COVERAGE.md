## 1. The defect this closes

0.7.4c2 gave the audit-backed source a fix that was correct and lossy at the same time. Its
completeness model required that every verified event became a fact, so that a filtered event could
not be read as a missing decision — and the same condition meant a stream carrying a *legitimate*
non-decision event (a DLP redaction, an approval lifecycle step) lost its verified range as well.

Reproduced on the mission's mixed stream before any production change was written: a stream of
`POLICY ALLOW`, `DLP REDACTED`, `POLICY DENY` produced projected positions `[1, 3]`, `assertedRange =
null`, and decision completeness `UNKNOWN`. The range had been verified, chain-consistent and
anchored to the head, and it was discarded because the field carrying it was also the denominator of
a decision-coverage calculation it cannot serve.

One further fact came out of the audit and is worth stating: with 0.7.4c2 in place,
`Completeness.GAPS_PRESENT` was **unreachable** from this source. A non-null range implied every
verified event was a record, which implied the records covered the span. The vocabulary could not
express "verified stream, one legitimate non-decision" at all.

## 2. The contract

One field, one claim. `DecisionFactRead.coverage` is a `StreamCoverage`, and
`DecisionProjection.verifiedStreamRange` carries the raw claim through to the caller.

**Raw-stream coverage** — `StreamCoverage.verifiedRange`: positions read from 1 contiguously, chain
verified over exactly those events, anchored to the observed head's position **and** hash with the
head re-read after the pages. A bounded read, a raced append, a foreign chain, a wrong stream or a
cross-run event carries no range, exactly as before.

**Decision classification coverage** — recorded next to it, per verified position:

| Outcome | Meaning | Effect on the decision claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| became a record | recognised as a policy decision or tool permission | accounts for the position |
| recognised non-decision | a producer in this repository records it in another family | accounts for it, never a gap |
| decision outcome, no record | recorded a decision outcome, produced no record | `GAPS_PRESENT` |
| unclassified | no producer in this repository is known to record it | `UNKNOWN` |

Recognition is a value match against the producers' own vocabulary:
`AuditEngineDlpRedactionAuditEmitter` (`DLP_MODEL_OUTPUT`, `DLP_TOOL_RESULT`, `REDACTED`) and
`AuditEngineApprovalLifecycleAuditEmitter` (its eight named lifecycle points and outcomes). An
emitter that changes its vocabulary stops being recognised, which withholds the claim rather than
granting it — the table cannot rot into a false COMPLETE.

**The fold proves the partition instead of trusting it.** `completenessOf` requires
`accountedPositions` to equal the span of the verified range before it will report `COMPLETE`, and it
rejects any record without a position or outside the range. Without that, "COMPLETE" would rest on the
source's buckets being exhaustive — the same silent drop, one level up. The count is O(1) arithmetic
on the range, no position enumeration.

### Documented meanings, so the two cannot be confused

- **COMPLETE** — every position in the verified range that this read was required to classify produced
  a record, and the read accounts for the whole range. It is a statement about **persisted records**.
  It is never evidence that no decision occurred: emission of these records is optional in this
  repository, which is why absence keeps `NOT_OBSERVED` and why the record set being complete says
  nothing about the runtime.
- **GAPS_PRESENT** — the verified range contains a position that recorded a decision outcome yet
  produced no record. A hole in the record set, not a filter.
- **UNKNOWN** — no range was verified, the range holds an unclassified event, a record carries no
  position or lies outside the range, the record set is empty, or the read did not account for the
  range it verified.

The invariant `COMPLETE ⟹ verifiedStreamRange != null` is kept, and it is one-way: the raw claim
never implies the decision claim, and the decision claim never implies the raw one.

### Rejected alternatives

- **Relax `projectedCount == events.size`** — reintroduces the 0.7.4c2 round-2 defect by
  construction: a redaction becomes a gap.
- **Count non-decision events and subtract them from the span** — the same defect with an extra step,
  and it silently treats every non-decision as known.
- **Per-family completeness from each family's positions** — the stream is shared and interleaved, so
  another family's position is not a hole in this family's history. Preserved from 0.7.4c1; it is why
  completeness is documented as a record-set claim.
- **Trusting the source's classification buckets without an account** — rejected during
  implementation, not in design: it leaves a source free to drop a position silently and still get
  COMPLETE. `accountedPositions` closes it, and mutation K7 shows the guard is load-bearing.
- **A public coverage type, or a second completeness enum** — the task forbids the first, and two
  statuses for one question invites the confusion the task warns about. The raw claim is a range with
  its anchoring conditions, which is already precise.

## 3. Changed production boundaries

Internal to `:tramai-security`, package `dev.tramai.security.governance`:

- `GovernanceProjection.kt` — `StreamCoverage` added; `DecisionFactRead.assertedRange` →
  `coverage`; `DecisionProjection.coverage` → `verifiedStreamRange`; `Completeness` documented.
- `GovernanceFactClassification.kt` — `completenessOf` takes the coverage and computes the record
  claim from the classification, proving the partition.
- `GovernanceProjectionReader.kt` — passes the coverage through; the source interface documents the
  coverage it receives.
- `GovernanceDecisionFold.kt` — carries `verifiedStreamRange` instead of an unqualified `coverage`.
- `AuditBackedGovernanceDecisionFactSource.kt` — one pass over the verified events produces the
  records and the three classification buckets; the producer vocabulary lives beside the decision
  vocabulary; `attestedRange` becomes `verifiedRange` and loses the filtered-read condition it no
  longer needs.

Unchanged: the `AuditStore` SPI and every store implementation, `AuditChainVerifier`, the registration
authority and `WorkloadControlPlaneQueries`, policy evaluation, enforcement semantics, the
`runtime-evidence.v1` schema, and the public API (no type here is public; the module `.api` dump is
untouched).

## 5. Production integration proof

Real audit-to-projection path, with no substitute at the audit boundary: `emitPolicy` /
`emitTool` → `AuditEnginePolicyDecisionAuditEmitter` → `AuditEngine` → `AuditStore.appendNext` in
`InMemoryAuditStore` → `AuditBackedGovernanceDecisionFactSource` → `GovernanceProjectionReader` →
projection against the real `WorkloadRegistrationAuthority`.

Verified there: a persisted policy DENY and ALLOW reach the policy family at their own source
positions; a tool permission lands in the tool family; `ALLOW` stays a `POLICY_AUTHORIZATION_RECORD`;
decision history stays independent of current registration version; the mixed stream reports
`1..3` with `COMPLETE`; the same stream with an unrecognised event reports `1..3` with `UNKNOWN`.

Durable store: `JdbcAuditStore` exists in `:tramai-persistence-jdbc` and shares the store TCK, but the
coverage proof was **not** repeated against it in this slice — see §8.

## 8. Unresolved limitations

- **Durable-store coverage proof not run.** The proof is against `InMemoryAuditStore`. `JdbcAuditStore`
  shares the TCK, but its read semantics under a real transaction boundary were not exercised here.
- **Non-decisions are recognised by value, not by declaration.** The table lists the values the
  producers record; if an emitter changes one, its events become unclassified and completeness is
  withheld. A producer-declared family marker would be stronger, and is not in this slice.
- **Approval lifecycle events are treated as non-decisions.** They stay visible in the raw range and
  are never projected, which is correct for the governance families as specified; a future
  approval-outcome family would need its own classification.
- **The decision claim is stream-level, reported per family**, as in 0.7.4c1. It does not say which
  positions *should* have carried a tool permission rather than a policy decision.
- **Unchanged and unresolved from earlier slices:** T1 workload identity authentication, event-origin
  authenticity (S19), live `provider.route` production (`TASK-0.7.4b7`), bundle-backed reads.

## 9. Non-claims

- No claim that an audit stream covers every runtime decision: emission is optional, and the authority
  vocabulary still reports `NOT_OBSERVED` for a family with no records.
- No claim that a verified range proves event-origin authenticity. `AuditChainVerifier` proves internal
  consistency only, and the declared boundary is never upgraded to a registered strength.
- No claim about execution or completion: `ALLOW` remains an authorization record.
- No claim that the projection can authorize, approve or enforce anything: it is read-only by
  construction, which its discriminators assert rather than trust.
- No Dashboard, HTTP endpoint, materialised store or background refresh was added.

## 10. Next dependency for 0.7.4d — materialised read model

0.7.4d can now materialise **two facts with different staleness requirements** instead of one
ambiguous coverage field: the verified raw range (a stream property, cheap to cache with the head it
was anchored to, invalidated by any append) and the decision-record coverage (a record-set property,
which a materialised view must recompute when its filter or recognition vocabulary changes). The
storage design has to keep them separable, because a cache that reuses one for the other reintroduces
exactly the collapse this slice removed.
