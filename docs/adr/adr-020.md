# ADR-020: Stage Governance Vocabulary Before Its Producer

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-04
- **Supersedes:** None
- **Related:** ADR-019 (reason over the authoritative governance decision type)

---

## Context

Governance work arrives in stages: authorization, viability, selection. Each
stage has its own outcome vocabulary, and a later stage refuses for reasons an
earlier stage cannot express.

Publishing a vocabulary before any code produces it is normally a defect: it
creates speculative API surface that reviewers must defend without a consumer to
justify it. Here the staging is deliberate. The alternative — declaring the
authorization and viability outcomes together, once viability has a producer —
means the viability vocabulary is designed while the authorization producer is
being written, which is how two stages get merged into one enum and stop being
separable.

## Decision

**A governance stage's outcome vocabulary may be declared before its producer,
on the condition that the vocabulary is not expanded opportunistically before
that producer lands.**

Consequences:

- The declaration states plainly that nothing produces the values yet, and why
  they are declared anyway.
- The later stage's vocabulary is a distinct type. This makes "no decision of
  this kind was performed" a structural property: the earlier boundary cannot
  return the later stage's outcome, so the staging cannot quietly collapse.
- **The first real producer either proves the model or exposes something
  genuinely missing.** Hypothetical states are not added because they might be
  needed. A vocabulary member without a producer is speculative surface; a
  vocabulary member that a producer cannot express is a gap the producer reveals.
- Optimization signals stay out of viability vocabulary on the same reasoning
  they stay out of authority: cost, latency and preference may rank candidates
  but may never remove one from the viable set.

## Status of the current instance

`CandidateViabilityDecision` and `ViabilityRefusal` are declared with no producer
(0.7.3b). The condition above is what keeps that acceptable: they are closed
until viability is actually evaluated, and the next viability slice either
confirms the three constraint families or shows which one is wrong.

## Evidence

- The vocabulary has no call site outside its declaration and its test.
- `CandidateAuthorization` returns `CandidateAuthorizationDecision`, which has no
  viability member: the boundary cannot perform a viability decision at all.
