# ADR-019: Reason Over the Authoritative Governance Decision Type

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-04
- **Supersedes:** None
- **Related:** ADR-010 (explicit provider registry, not model-prefix routing), ADR-018 (security enforcement separated from SaaS platform concerns)

---

## Context

The 0.7.3 authorized-selection epic establishes governance as typed stages:
authorization, then viability, then selection. Its first slice exposed
authorization as a `Boolean`. A refusal then had no cause, and a caller could not
tell "denied" from "not yet decided" — the distinction existed in the model but
not in the answer.

A boolean projection of a governance decision looks harmless while only two
outcomes exist. It stops being harmless as soon as richer authority semantics
arrive — `ALLOW`, `DENY`, `REQUIRE_APPROVAL`, `DEFER`, `NOT_APPLICABLE` — because
every one of them projects onto `true` or `false`. A convenience accessor such as
`decision.authorizes()` lets a consumer keep reasoning in that collapsed space
while the runtime depends on distinctions the boolean cannot carry. On a
governance boundary that is accidental policy loss, and it is invisible: the call
site compiles, reads correctly, and passes review.

## Decision

**Consumers must reason over the authoritative decision type rather than
projecting governance decisions into a boolean authorization result.**

Consequences:

- A governance boundary returns a decision type as its only answer. Refusals
  carry a member of a stable, closed reason family, so a caller branches on the
  decision instead of re-deriving meaning from a truth value.
- Boolean-shaped conveniences over a decision are not added. When the typed
  decision landed, `CandidateAuthorization.authorizes(): Boolean` was **removed**
  rather than deprecated or kept as a derived accessor.
- The removal is declared as a removal in `config/quality/api-migrations.yml`,
  with its consumer impact, rather than buried inside an "additive only"
  rationale.
- Adding an outcome is a source change that breaks compilation at every call
  site that must handle it. That is intended: a new authority outcome cannot be
  silently absorbed by an existing default arm.

## What this does not decide

A predicate is not a decision. `ProviderInputRelease.releases(...)` stays a
`Boolean`, because it answers "does this input go out" at a release boundary
rather than reporting governance authority. Where a boundary's answer *is* the
authority, the decision type is the answer.

## Evidence

- `CandidateAuthorizationDecision` — `Authorized | NotAuthorized(reason)`,
  `authorizes()` removed, `decisionFor()` in its place (0.7.3b).
- `AuthorizationRefusal` — the closed refusal family.
- The public API dump records zero occurrences of `authorizes(`.
